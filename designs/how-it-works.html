No cart. No fees.
Just the farm, and you.

The rules, in plain words

Short on purpose. Break them and the listing comes down.
Producers onlyYou made it, grew it, raised it or caught it — or you're the farm that did. No resold, imported or dropshipped goods. Resellers get removed.
Verified means a person lookedOne document, reviewed by our team. It confirms who you are and that you're registered. It is not an inspection and we don't pretend it is.
We never handle moneyNo cart, no checkout, no escrow. You pay the seller the way they take it. Disputes are between you and them, like at any farm stand.
Local means localSearch results are within your radius, period. Shippers have their own page. National retailers are labeled Sponsored and never appear in local results.
Prepared food is allowed in every stateHome cooks list with their permit status shown: "Permitted" or "Home kitchen · no permit on file." You decide.
Neighbor shares are freeBackyard surplus — fruit, eggs, cuttings. No account, block-level location, expires in 14 days. Not for resale.

Questions people ask

Is this a marketplace? Can I check out here?

No. Farm to Local is a directory. You find the seller here and buy from them the way they already sell — stand, market, their site, a phone call. We never take a payment or a cut of it.

What does "Verified" actually mean?

A person on our team looked at one document — a state registration, a market vendor letter, a permit — and confirmed the seller is who they say they are. It is not a farm inspection and it is not a food-safety certification.

How do you make money if it's free?

Sellers who want more reach pay for Pro. Bulk sellers pay a small commission on sales we track. National retailers pay for labeled sponsored slots. Gear recommendations earn affiliate commission. Buyers never pay anything.

Why is my farm listed and I never signed up?

We seed listings from public registries and market rosters so buyers see a full map on day one. It's name, town, category, market — nothing private. Claim it and it's yours; ask and we remove it.

Can I list prepared food from my home kitchen?

Yes, in every state. Your listing shows your permit status — "Permitted" or "Home kitchen · no permit on file" — and buyers decide. You're responsible for your state's cottage-food and MEHKO rules.

Something went wrong with a seller. What do you do?

Report the listing. A person reviews it within 48 hours. Rule-breakers — resellers, no-shows, misrepresented products — come down. Money disputes are between you and the seller, same as any farm stand.

How local is "local"?

Your search radius, which you set: 25, 50 or 100 miles. Results are sorted by distance. Sellers who ship are on their own page so they never crowd out the farm down the road.
